# The dsh wire client knows no pack nouns; the pack's dsh policy lives beside it

The dsh runner is two modules. `dsh-runtime.ts` is protocol and nothing else: spawn `dsh`, speak newline-delimited JSON-RPC (`initialize`, `session/prompt`, `shutdown`), follow `session.event` and `session.status` for its own session id, resolve a turn on `idle` after the turn has started, then report `finishReason()`/`lastMessage()` and close (shutdown, then SIGTERM, then SIGKILL). Its constructor takes `{cwd, env, argv, provider, model, effort}` and it names no role, ticket, persona or artifact. `dsh-agent.ts` is the pack's dsh policy: the `sdk-minimal` profile, the deepseek provider default, the effort fold from the pack's seven thinking levels onto dsh's four (`off`, `low`, `high`, `max`, everything between folding to `high`), credentials (environment first, then `providers.packy` in `~/.pi/agent/models.json`), `DSH_HOME` (`~/.dsh-pack`), where dsh keeps its session log, and the wall-clock kill. Where the two runners differ is stated once, in the adapter's doc block: the effort fold, the absent cancel (a wall-clock `SIGKILL`), and that dsh's log is never read back - the answer travels on the seam.

The split buys a test that needs neither credentials nor a persona: `tests/dsh-agent-repro.ts` drives `DshRuntime` alone against a stub `dsh` and asserts the wire (argv, initialize params, session id, the idle rule, the strict `type === "text"` answer filter), while the adapter is tested for the policy it adds on top.

Rejected: the python SDK or a venv (the protocol is small enough for bun to speak directly, and the pack otherwise has no runtime dependency); one `dsh-agent.ts` holding both (305 lines in which a reader could not tell what the harness guarantees from what the pack chose); a shared "harness" abstraction over Pi and dsh (they share a seam, not an implementation); reading dsh's `session.v3.jsonl` back as the answer (that file is the runtime's product, not the seam's channel).
